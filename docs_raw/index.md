# Open Vitals

An ubiquitous framework for defining, collecting, and exchanging medical observational data. While [FHIR7](https://www.hl7.org/fhir/overview.html) is a widely adopted standard for healthcare data exchange, Open Vitals aims to provide a more flexible and standardized approach to collecting, storing and tracking medical data.

## [What is Open Vitals?](#what)

Open Vitals is an open-source framework that provides a standardized approach to collecting, storing, and tracking medical data across different sources. It is designed to be flexible and adaptable to different healthcare settings and use cases. In the context of healthcare, this approach must allow patients to have a better understanding of their health status and track their progress over time.

## [Why Open Vitals?](#why)

The proliferation of medical devices, wearables, and digital health applications has led to an exponential increase in the generation of diverse medical data. While countries, private organizations, and healthcare providers develop internal systems to manage patients' health data, people still face challenges in accessing and managing their own health data across different sources due to the following reasons:

**Diverged report formats**. Different vendors or healthcare providers often provides disorganized health data focused on a particular use case. It can lead to data loss, duplication, and inconsistencies over a long period of time.

**Lack of interoperability**. Although, healthcare or wellness providers can manage electronic health records of a particular patient to some extent, they are having the following limitations:

- Limited access due to proprietary infrastructure
- Overcomplicated data access process
- Inconsistent formats between different vendors or healthcare providers
- Lack of available tooling for efficient medical data exploration

It leads to that patients often struggle to collect and manage the data at length. Reports, by their nature, represent a fragmented snapshot of the patient's health status with no prior track of records collected up to a certain point. Thus it hinders the trust between the patient and healthcare professionals leading to all kinds of issues:

- Challenges in health data interpretation
- Inefficient communication between parties
- Lack of data precision during patient examination

**Lack of traceability**. When it comes to multiple health conditions or ones which hard to diagnose, electronic health records don't have any traceable connections between steps made during examinations within or across the medical institutions leading to all sorts of medical challenges in tracing the patient's health journey across over the long period of time. This standard also outlines a governance model for its evolution and provides recommendations for secure and compliant implementations.

**No control over personal data**. Electronic health records often contain sensitive information about patients, such as their medical history, diagnosis, and treatment plans. However, there is no standard way to ensure that patients have control over their personal data, including the ability to access, review, and share some portion of their health records.

## Goal
To provide a standard of organizing electronic health records that ensures interoperability, traceability, and patient control over their personal data.
